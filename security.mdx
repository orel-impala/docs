## What Impala does with your content

**Prompts are not persisted.** Inputs and outputs are processed to serve your request. They are not retained as prompt data.

**Your content is never used for training.** Not for model training, not for fine-tuning, not for model improvement.

**Telemetry describes the workload, not the content.** What Impala retains and acts on is workload shape — prompt lengths, concurrency, burstiness, cache reuse. That's what [Herd](/impala-herd) profiles to tune the execution plan. It is not your prompts or completions.

## Where content lives

Batch necessarily involves a file: you upload a JSONL and retrieve a results file. Where those files sit depends on your deployment.

| | Serverless | Bring your own cloud |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Batch input and output files | Impala-managed storage, for the life of the batch | Your own S3 buckets |
| Content leaves your cloud | Yes, to Impala's endpoint | No |
| Network path | Public endpoint, authenticated | Private, over AWS PrivateLink |

**Bring your own cloud is the option where content never leaves your account.** The data plane runs inside your VPC, reads and writes your buckets, and reaches the endpoint over PrivateLink rather than the public internet. Batch runs in both models — a VPC install is not a prerequisite for batch. See [Run in our cloud, or yours](/deployment-options).

## Isolation

Deployments are **single-tenant**. Your deployment is logically isolated from other customers, in Impala's cloud or in yours. In a Bring your own cloud install, the control plane and data plane are split, with the data plane inside your VPC.

On-premises deployment is supported, subject to compatibility testing by Impala's solutions team.

## Authentication

Requests carry a bearer token. The same credential authenticates the OpenAI-compatible inference endpoints and Impala-specific operations such as [Impala Leap](/weight-sync). Keys are issued per account — see [Get your credentials](/impala-cloud-setup).

If you front Impala with a gateway such as [LiteLLM](/connect-litellm), the Impala key lives in the proxy's environment and your clients present a separate key to the proxy. That is the point of running one.

## Compliance and testing

Impala is certified under **SOC 2 Type II**, covering controls for security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy.

The platform is security-tested on a regular cadence, including vulnerability assessments and penetration testing.

Current attestations, control descriptions and the subprocessor list are in the [Trust Center](https://trust.getimpala.ai). Impala's [security overview](https://www.getimpala.ai/security) covers the same posture at a higher level.

## Questions this page doesn't answer

For anything specific to your environment — data residency in a particular region, a signed DPA, a custom retention requirement, or a security questionnaire — talk to your Impala contact.
